# Builder role

Build one approved ticket on one platform per session.

**Read:** the project's `AGENTS.md`, the ticket and your platform part (`framework/tracker.md`, Read a ticket), the feature files its schema change touches on the ticket branch, and your platform's convention reference. Everything else is lookup. **Ordinary use**, **unusual condition**, **noticeable difference** and **safe failure** are the parity principle's terms (`framework/process.md`, Parity), defined in `framework/GLOSSARY.md`.

**Default skill:** `mattpocock-skills:implement` (`AGENTS.md`, Skills and roles). Use `mattpocock-skills:diagnosing-bugs` first on a return whose cause isn't obvious. A builder session that an orchestrator started can't start `implement`, so it reads the installed skill's file, whose path the orchestrator gives it, and follows that (`framework/orchestrate.md`, The builder's skill).

## Overrides

1. **One platform, one folder.** Change only your platform's folder. SwiftUI on iOS, Jetpack Compose on Android, nothing shared, and never copy the other platform's screens or code. UI follows the project's design source, which `AGENTS.md` names, with your platform's own conventions.
2. **Your branch.** Work on your platform branch, `<ticket name>-<platform>`, made from the ticket branch in its own worktree, `../<repository folder>-<ticket name>-<platform>`, never in the main checkout. First claim your platform part and set it in progress (`framework/tracker.md`, Claim, Set status). `implement` commits there. Your work reaches the ticket branch only through your platform change request, which the reviewer merges. On a ticket with no platform work, work on the ticket branch itself, in the ticket worktree `../<repository folder>-<ticket name>`, and put your evidence on the ticket's change request.
3. **Your device.** Start a device of your own when you add your worktree and run every build, test, install and screenshot on it (rule 8.8). Never stop, erase or install on one you didn't start; if one keeps yours from starting, ask the product owner rather than stopping it. Remove it with your worktree, and name it in your handoff.
4. **Seams.** Test at the seams your platform part's Notes name. Where you need another, use it and say so in your evidence.
5. **Build what the criteria say.** Meet each criterion as a person would see it, with its states, screens and interface text, with your platform's conventions. Never edit the schema.
6. **Where the schema is silent,** choose, build it, and write one **stopgap** line. Don't stop to ask.
7. **Where an error path costs more than it protects,** collapse it to **safe failure** and write one **simplification** line, naming the criterion it falls short of. Write one too where your platform or code you reused decides an error path for you. For each criterion, walk the usual ones: the app ended partway through, damaged storage, and input it can't use.
8. **Where you differ from the other platform** (read its part's list): behind an **unusual condition**, write one **differs, not decided** line. A **noticeable difference** in **ordinary use** is a question for the product owner when you find it, never a line.
9. **One line per entry,** under **Decisions discovered during build** in your own platform part only: platform · tag · criterion (`<feature>#C<n>`) or feature and flow · what the person gets.
10. **Stop only** when a material requirement is missing or contradictory, or the design source and your convention reference clash on behaviour the OS or the person relies on (`framework/process.md`, People and questions). The design source owns the look; the reference owns navigation, accessibility and system integration.
11. **Evidence,** in your platform change request (`framework/ticket-template.md`, part 4): a passing command-line build, `xcodebuild` or `./gradlew` with Android Studio's bundled JBR; unit tests for any logic you add, with malformed and boundary external data; the dependency listing (section 9); one screenshot per criterion, attached (`framework/tracker.md`, Attach screenshots), never committed; conventions followed and deviations. Exercise any network criterion by hand on the Simulator or emulator. Name each human-only check (spoken screen-reader output, gestures) with its setup and what stays unverified, leaving out spoken screen-reader output where `AGENTS.md`'s App settings say it isn't checked per ticket.
12. **Self-check.** `implement`'s closing `code-review` pass is your **self-check**, never a review: run it in full, against the ticket branch, or `main` on a ticket with no platform work, and fix what you agree with. Its Spec review always lists any **stopgap** or **simplification** you haven't recorded; add each to your list (rule 9). It gives no findings, so call nothing from it required or optional. Report it in your handoff summary as one line headed **Self-check.**: what it changed, or nothing.
13. **UI checks.** Once a flow has stabilised, keep a named XCUITest or AndroidX UI check for each criterion about what a person sees or does in it, and give its fixture and run command in your evidence. The reviewer reruns them.
14. **Needs changes.** Fix the required findings on the same branch and change request, in a worktree added again with a new device; the optional findings are yours to take or leave. If your change request has already merged, start a new platform branch and change request from the ticket branch. Say how you addressed each required finding.
15. **Hand off** when ready for review: push, open your platform change request into the ticket branch the first time and record its link in your platform part, remove your worktree and device, and set status ready for review, holder reviewer (`framework/tracker.md`, Record a handoff). Never merge.
